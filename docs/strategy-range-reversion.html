GROUP 3 · AMZN · GOOGL

Range Reversion

Fade statistical washouts at the lower band while the long-term trend holds, sell the snap-back to the mean, never overstay. Script: pine-scripts/03_range_reversion_platforms.pine

Why this bucket trades differently

AMZN and GOOGL are structurally great businesses trading under overhangs — known unknowns that cap the multiple until a court, a regulator or an earnings print resolves them. For GOOGL it's antitrust: U.S. rulings on search and ad-tech, a €4.1B EU fine upheld, an India ads battle — even as Google Cloud grows 63% Y/Y and the stock sits +15% YTD after a +107% trailing-year re-rate. For AMZN it's the capex math: ~$200B pouring into AI infrastructure has collapsed trailing free cash flow ~95%, and at +6.5% YTD the market has parked the stock until the AWS re-acceleration (28% growth, 37.7% margin) visibly outruns the spend.

The result is a distinctive price signature: long sideways coils punctuated by step-function re-rates when the fog lifts, with headline-driven overshoots inside the range. Momentum breakouts routinely stall into the next overhang headline; buy-and-hold ties up capital for quarters. What works is the oscillation itself: when one of these names washes out to the bottom of its volatility envelope while its 200-day trend is still rising, the odds meaningfully favor a snap-back to the mean within days.

The edge, in one sentence Overhang stocks oscillate around value between re-rates — so the strategy buys statistical panic (lower Bollinger Band + fast-RSI washout) only above a rising long-term trend, and sells the return to the mean rather than hoping for a breakout.

The rules

Structural gate — only fade dips in intact stories Price must be above the 200-day SMA. Mean reversion below a broken long-term trend is how reversion accounts die; if the overhang genuinely re-rates the stock down, this gate removes the strategy from the field.
Setup — a statistical washout, not a mild dip Close below the lower Bollinger Band (20, 2.0) and RSI(3) below 20. Requiring both means price is stretched versus its own recent volatility and the last three days were genuinely one-sided selling.
Exit — the snap-back IS the trade Close the position when price closes back above the 20-day basis. No trailing, no hoping it becomes a trend trade. Reversion pays by being paid often, not big.
Time stop — stale washouts are dead washouts If the bounce hasn't arrived within 10 bars, exit flat. A washout that doesn't bounce in two weeks is usually the market slowly repricing the overhang — not your trade.
Disaster stop — for when the dip is a re-rating Hard stop at entry − 3 × ATR(14). Deliberately wide (reversion entries are early by construction); it exists for the adverse-ruling or guidance-cut scenario, not for noise.

Tuning guidance for this regime

  • Earnings (GOOGL Jul 27, AMZN Jul 30): the script has no earnings calendar. The professional habit is to skip fresh entries in the 2–3 sessions before a print — a washout into earnings is a coin-flip on the report, not a statistical edge.
  • Antitrust decision days are the GOOGL-specific version of the same rule: remedy rulings can re-rate the stock in either direction and invalidate the mean.
  • If the Fed hikes and the index corrects, the 200-day gate will start rejecting AMZN first (it has the least YTD cushion). Expected behavior — don't loosen the gate to "get more trades."
  • Higher-conviction variant: 2.5σ bands + RSI(2) < 10, exit at basis. Roughly half the trades, noticeably better average trade.

How it fails, and what protects you

Failure modes
  • The dip that isn't a dip: an adverse antitrust remedy or an AWS growth miss turns a washout into a step-function re-rate down. Protection: the disaster stop caps the single-trade loss, and the 200-day gate stops the strategy from fading the subsequent downtrend.
  • Grinding descents: a slow bleed can tag the lower band repeatedly without ever producing the bounce. Protection: the time stop recycles capital out of dead trades, and each re-entry needs a fresh band + RSI washout.
  • Regime flip to trend: if an overhang resolves bullishly, the stock re-rates and leaves the range — and this strategy exits at the mean, missing the run. Accepted cost: that upside belongs to the breakout playbook, not this one. No single script should try to be both.
