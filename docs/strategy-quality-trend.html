GROUP 1 · MSFT · AAPL

Quality Trend Pullback

Buy orderly dips inside a confirmed uptrend, hold until the trend actually cracks. Script: pine-scripts/01_quality_trend_pullback_megacap.pine

Why this bucket trades differently

MSFT and AAPL are the market's ballast: enormous float, deep institutional ownership, relentless buybacks, and a permanent bid from passive index flows. They almost never lead a rally, and they almost never crash without weeks of deterioration first. Their charts are staircases — trend, shallow pullback, trend — because every dip is met by systematic buyers who must own them.

In the July 2026 regime this character is amplified. MSFT heads into its Jul 29 report as the market's execution benchmark (anything under ~35% Azure growth reads as deceleration, but consensus targets near $590 say the street still believes). AAPL is +20% YTD precisely because its capital-light AI strategy — monetizing 2.3B devices instead of building data centers — insulates it from the capex anxiety hammering the rest of the complex. When hawkish-Fed or Iran headlines knock the index, these are the names whose dips are shallowest and get bought first.

The edge, in one sentence In structurally-bid compounders, a pullback inside an intact uptrend is a discount, not a warning — so the strategy buys the reset and refuses to sell until the trend itself breaks.

The rules

Regime gate — is the staircase intact? Price must be above the 200-day SMA and the 50-day must sit above the 200-day. No stack, no trades. This alone keeps the strategy out of genuine bear phases.
Setup — an orderly reset RSI(14) dips below 45 at some point in the last 10 bars. That's a normal breather in a strong mega-cap; RSI(14) at 30 in these names usually means something is actually wrong.
Trigger — buyers back in control RSI is back above the threshold and the bar closes above the prior bar's high. You're not catching the knife; you're joining the resumption.
Initial protection Stop at entry − 2.5 × ATR(14). Deliberately wide: tight stops in low-noise names get clipped by noise and then watch the move leave without you.
Exit — only when the trend cracks Close below the 50-day EMA (armed only after price has traded above it during the position), or the ATR stop. Optional R-multiple profit target, OFF by default — the whole point of this bucket is letting winners compound for weeks to months.

Tuning guidance for this regime

  • If the Fed actually hikes in September and the index corrects, the 200-day gate will start rejecting entries on its own. Don't override it.
  • Around the Jul 29–30 earnings prints, consider standing aside on new entries for 2–3 days. The strategy has no earnings calendar — that judgment stays manual.
  • Want more trades? Lower the RSI threshold to 40 and the exit EMA to 21. Expect more churn and smaller average winners.
  • Want fewer, higher-quality trades? Raise the pullback threshold's strictness by requiring RSI < 40 and keep everything else default.

How it fails, and what protects you

Failure modes
  • Regime break: a hawkish-pivot bear market turns "buy the dip" into "catch every knife." Protection: the 200-day gate stops issuing entries, and the 50-EMA exit gets you out of open positions early in the decline.
  • Earnings gap through the stop: an Azure miss could gap MSFT below the ATR stop. Protection is partial (stops fill at the open, not the stop price) — which is why this bucket uses moderate default sizing rather than max leverage.
  • Slow bleed: a months-long distribution that never triggers the pullback reset. The strategy simply stays flat — being in cash is a position.
