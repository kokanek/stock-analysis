GROUP 2 · NVDA · AMD · MU

High-Beta Momentum Breakout

Buy confirmed strength with volume, size the position off volatility, bank half at +2R, trail the rest. Script: pine-scripts/02_momentum_breakout_semis.pine

Why this bucket trades differently

The AI-semi complex is where the market expresses both its greed and its fear about the AI buildout, at maximum leverage. The dispersion tells the story: AMD is up ~150% YTD as its MI-series accelerators win hyperscaler sockets; NVDA is up just ~13% — still the ~70–80% share king, but priced at ~43× forward with nothing left for error; MU is riding an HBM-driven memory super-cycle at a discount multiple. These stocks don't drift — they impulse: multi-week runs that start from breakouts, punctuated by brutal air pockets like the mid-July session where capex-cut fears took the whole group down and the Nasdaq lost 1.5% in a day.

Layer on the geopolitics: Taiwan is not a headline risk for semis, it is the supply chain. Export-control announcements, blockade escalations and hyperscaler capex remarks all land as overnight gaps. Buying dips blindly in names that can re-rate −20% on one policy headline is how accounts get destroyed. The historically robust approach in high-beta momentum names is the opposite: only buy strength that proves itself, and make every trade survivable by construction.

The edge, in one sentence In crowd-driven momentum names, a fresh N-day high on expanding volume tends to keep going short-term — so the strategy buys exactly that, and spends all its sophistication on the exit and the position size, which is where high-beta trading is actually won.

The rules

Regime gate — stock AND market The stock must be above its 100-day SMA, and (by default) SPY must be above its 50-day. Breakouts fail at far higher rates when the index is rolling over on Fed repricing — the market filter keeps you from buying strength into an index air pocket.
Trigger — proven strength Close above the prior 20-day high with volume ≥ 1.2× its 20-day average. Price says momentum; volume says participation. Both or nothing.
Size — volatility-budgeted Position size = 1% of equity ÷ (2 × ATR stop distance), capped at 25% of equity notional. A quiet MU and a wild AMD get the same dollar risk, not the same share count. This is the single most important line in the script.
Bank the first leg Sell 50% at +2R (default ON). In a tape where the group can give back a week of gains in a day on one capex headline, taking the fast money materially smooths the equity curve.
Trail the rest — and obey the eject handle Chandelier stop: 22-bar high − 3 × ATR, ratcheting up only. Separately, a close back below the 100-day SMA force-exits everything — that's the "the thesis is wrong" escape hatch, not a suggestion.

Tuning guidance for this regime

  • After the mid-July capex scare, expect the market filter and 100-day gate to reject entries for a while if weakness persists. That's the design working — momentum systems make their money by being absent during resets.
  • MU vs NVDA/AMD: MU is the most cyclical of the three; some traders run it with Donchian 55 (position-trade the memory cycle) while keeping 20 for NVDA/AMD.
  • If Taiwan/Iran risk escalates further, cut "risk per trade" to 0.5% before touching anything else. Sizing is the lever that saves you, not smarter entries.
  • In a confirmed melt-up (index and group above all gates, breadth improving), turning the scale-out off lets winners run to the chandelier — greedier, streakier.

How it fails, and what protects you

Failure modes
  • Whipsaw clusters: choppy ranges produce breakout → stop → breakout → stop sequences. Protection: the volume filter trims fake breakouts, and 1% risk per trade means a losing streak is an annoyance, not an event.
  • Gap through the stop: an export-control or blockade headline can open a semi −15%, far below any stop. Protection: the sizing formula — a 1%-risk position that gaps 3× past its stop still only costs ~3% of equity.
  • Capex-cycle turn: if hyperscalers genuinely cut, every gate in this script closes and it goes flat. It will not short the decline — that's a different strategy for a different repo.
